Coursera: free credits and offers

Best offer on record: Course Preview (the 2025 replacement of 'audit mode') — free first module of most courses. 7 verified findings, checked 2026-10-22.

Offers

Course Preview (the 2025 replacement of 'audit mode') — free first module of most courses high confidence

Since ~Aug 8, 2025 the old unlimited audit experience was replaced by a 'preview' model: for most courses you get free full access to the FIRST module only (video lectures, readings, that module's graded assignments, and Coursera Coach where available). All later modules lock until you upgrade. Per Coursera support, a preview can last up to 6 months. No certificate in preview mode; upgrading to the paid course (one-time, ~$49-79/month per course listing, 180-day access) or financial aid unlocks the certificate.

Eligibility
Open to anyone worldwide. No .edu/student email, no credit card, no signup payment required for the free preview itself. (Official support article learner-000002293, 'How to preview a course', Aug 28, 2025; blog.coursera.org Aug 8, 2025).
How to claim
Go to any course page > click 'Enroll'/'Start for free' > at the bottom of the enrollment dialog choose 'Preview this course'. For courses inside a Specialization/Professional Certificate, open the individual course page first. If no 'Preview'/'Full Course, No Certificate' link appears, that course has no free option.
Expiry
Preview access for a given course: up to 6 months per Coursera support; no renewal — afterwards you must pay or re-enroll.
Gotchas
Preview = first module only (not the old full-course audit); no certificate; 'Enroll for free' buttons inside Specializations actually start a paid 7-day trial subscription, not a free audit; if you came via an org program whose contract ended, you are switched to preview automatically.
Source
coursera.support

'Full Course, No Certificate' + partner-supported free access (incl. 'Free Community Impact Courses') high confidence

For select courses, Coursera offers 'Full Course, No Certificate': free access to ALL course materials and graded assessments, no certificate. Coursera's Aug 2025 blog also introduced 'Free Community Impact Courses' (mental health, refugee support, public health) with partner-supported free access to full course content; on those, a certificate can optionally be purchased afterwards. Third-party tracker (Class Central, Aug 2026) counts 470+ courses still fully free, and 6 with partner-supported free access incl. 'The Science of Well-Being'.

Eligibility
Open to anyone; no .edu, no credit card. Which courses qualify is course-by-course — look for the option in the enrollment dialog; coursera.org/explore/free-courses lists the free catalog ('Learn from Anthropic, AWS, Microsoft, Stanford, and more — free from start to finish').
How to claim
Open the course page > Enroll > choose 'Full Course, No Certificate' (small link under the paid buttons; inside a Specialization open the individual course page, not the program page). For partner-supported courses, Coursera says to use the FAQ link on the course page rather than 'Enroll for free' (per Class Central's writeup).
Expiry
No stated expiry; the free option persists as long as the course keeps the 'Full Course, No Certificate' designation — it can change if the partner withdraws the free offer.
Gotchas
Still no certificate (the point of the option); partner-supported access is a different mode: do NOT click 'Enroll for free' on those — follow the FAQ instruction or you may start a paid trial; the free-courses page itself also pushes a 7-day Coursera Plus trial for full access.
Source
coursera.org

7-day free trial: monthly Specializations/Professional Certificates and Coursera Plus high confidence

Coursera Plus (monthly $59, 'Save 40%' annual $399) gives unlimited access to 90%+ of the catalog (10,000+ courses/programs incl. capstone projects; degrees/MasterTrack excluded) and starts with a 7-day free trial. Most monthly course/Specialization subscriptions also start with a free trial (per 'Enrollment options' support article). Coursera captures payment info at signup; the card is charged only at the end of the 7-day trial. Plus certificates are unlimited per course completed. Free-access page FAQ confirms: 'Start a 7-day free trial for Specializations or Coursera Plus.'

Eligibility
Any individual account; no .edu or startup status required. A credit/payment method IS required (captured at signup, charged if trial lapses).
How to claim
Sign up > subscribe to a monthly Specialization/Prof. Cert or start Coursera Plus > complete any free-trial work immediately > cancel in Purchases before day 7 to avoid the charge.
Expiry
Trial: 7 days, then $59/mo (Plus monthly) or the course's monthly price. Annual Plus has no trial — see separate finding.
Gotchas
Trial-vs-free: cards are captured up front; you do NOT earn a Course Certificate during a free trial — certificates are only issued after the trial ends and the first payment is charged (official note in 'Get a Course Certificate'); pre-existing Specialization subscriptions are not auto-cancelled when you add Plus (double-charge risk); cancel-anytime applies only to the monthly plan.
Source
coursera.org

Coursera Plus annual: 14-day money-back window (not a true trial) high confidence

Annual Coursera Plus = $399/year (vs $59/mo monthly) with a 14-day money-back guarantee: pay upfront, request a refund via 'My Purchases' within 14 days. Coursera revokes any certificates earned within those first 14 days if you refund.

Eligibility
Any individual account; payment required up front; no .edu or startup requirement.
How to claim
Subscribe to annual Plus > if it's not for you, request the refund in Purchases within 14 days.
Expiry
Refund window: 14 days from purchase. After that, the year is paid and non-refundable.
Gotchas
This is a pay-then-refund window, not free access — you pay day one; certificates earned inside the window are clawed back on refund.
Source
coursera.org

Financial aid (per-course grants, can make certificates free) medium confidence

Most Coursera courses offer financial aid for learners who can't afford the course; it can unlock full course access AND the certificate for free (or at reduced cost), per the Aug 2025 preview-model announcement and the 'Enrollment options' help article. Per-course application with an essay; typical decision time ~15 days and a ~14-day access window (historical figures; Coursera's own help article 'Apply for financial aid' covers current rules). Not available on all course types (e.g., some degree-adjacent content).

Eligibility
Need-based, open to individuals worldwide — a .edu/student email is NOT required; students, working professionals, and anyone meeting the financial-need criteria can apply. No credit card needed.
How to claim
On the course page look for 'Financial aid available? Learn more' > submit the per-course application > wait for approval (historically ~15 days) > get a time-boxed window to complete the course and earn the certificate. You can preview/audit while awaiting approval, but graded submissions unlock on approval.
Expiry
Grant is per-course and time-boxed (~14 days from aid start, historical); does not cover whole Specializations automatically — apply per course; must be re-claimed for each course.
Gotchas
Paying for the course cancels a pending aid application (community-confirmed); aid does not usually cover program-level certificates/degrees; approval can take weeks, so plan around deadlines.
Source
coursera.support

Education license: Coursera for Campus / Coursera for Universities (institutional, no .edu tier for individuals) high confidence

Coursera for Campus is a B2B offering: partner universities (Imperial College London, University of Michigan, TU Munich, University of Melbourne, Manipal, NMIMS, Ivey Business School) license the platform (10,600+ courses from 350+ partners, Career Academy, Guided Projects, Professional Certificates, LMS integration) so their enrolled students get access. There is NO individual '.edu email = free access' tier: a student gets free access only if their own institution has a Campus plan. No per-seat or plan pricing is published; 'Compare Plans' / 'Contact us' only.

Eligibility
Institutions, not individuals. Students of partner universities receive access through the institution (typically .edu verification by the school's admin, not a self-serve .edu signup at coursera.org).
How to claim
Individual student: nothing to self-claim — ask whether your university licenses Coursera for Campus/Plus; institutions: Coursera sales (coursera.org/campus, 'Compare Plans').
Expiry
Tied to the institution's contract; when a contract ends or a learner is removed from the program, enrolled courses are switched to free preview mode (official 'Enrollment options' note).
Gotchas
No published free individual tier exists at the .edu level; if your school doesn't have the plan, the free paths are Preview / Full-Course-No-Certificate / financial aid / 7-day trials only; org-sourced access silently downgrades to preview on contract end.
Source
coursera.org

Startups: no Coursera startup program exists — only paid team/enterprise plans and the generic 7-day trial high confidence

coursera.org/startups, /plus and /startups return 404 — there is no official startup tier, no startup credits, no startup-specific education license. The business funnel is 'Coursera for Teams' (up to 499 employees) vs enterprise (4,700+ companies), behind 'Compare Plans'/'Contact sales' with zero pricing on the public page and no free tier or trial advertised. A founder's only free window is the same 7-day Coursera Plus trial available to anyone.

Eligibility
No startup age or funding cap exists because there is no startup program; team/enterprise plans are commercial, paid, sales-led.
How to claim
N/A — nothing to claim. For a small startup, the practical free options are: the 7-day Plus trial (single seat, credit card required), per-course financial aid, Preview / Full-Course-No-Certificate free access.
Expiry
N/A.
Gotchas
Do not confuse partner-funded 'community impact' free courses or a university's Campus plan with a startup offer; any 'Coursera for startups' content online is third-party, not official; Teams plans have no public pricing or trial, so budget for sales-quoted cost.
Source
coursera.org

2025–26 changes

  • Major 2025 sunset: Coursera retired the classic unlimited 'audit mode' (full free access to lectures of most courses, no certificate) around Aug 8, 2025, replacing it with the Preview experience — free access is now capped at the FIRST module of most courses; 'Full Course, No Certificate' survived only for a select subset (470+ courses per Class Central's Aug 2026 audit, ~2% of the 21,000+ catalog). Consequences: (1) free access shrank from 'whole course minus certificate' to 'first module only' for the majority of courses; (2) the free-trial period for subscriptions is 7 days (not the 30 days some older guides cite) with a card captured up front; (3) certificates are explicitly withheld during free trials; (4) localized/geographic (geo) pricing was introduced in 2025, so paid upgrade prices vary by region while the free tiers stay free; (5) a 'Free Community Impact Courses' program (mental health, refugee support, public health) was added in 2025 as a partner-supported free-access carve-out; (6) no .edu-only student tier and no startup program exist as of 2026-10-22 (verified 404s on /startups, /plus, /for-universities; free paths are Preview, Full-Course-No-Certificate, financial aid, 7-day trials, and institution-provided Campus access). No free offer was closed in 2026 that had existed in 2025 beyond the audit->preview shrinkage; financial aid remains offered.
